## A Word That Has Lost Its Meaning

Today, the word _racist_ has ceased to mean anything. It has been inflated until it deflates into an empty word — hurled at anyone who dares to disagree, to observe, or to think for themselves. It is the go-to silencer of modern discourse: “You’re a racist.” Discussion over. No need to engage with the argument, no need to offer evidence, no need to think. Just throw the label and walk away victorious.

But if _everything_ is racist, _nothing_ is. And when a word loses its meaning, truth loses its vocabulary.

So let’s clarify. Let’s distinguish. Let’s separate the real thing from the counterfeit.

## 1980s Racism

By “1980s racism” I mean the classic, unambiguous definition that every English speaker once understood. Racism is the prejudice, hostility, discrimination, or antagonism directed against a person because of their racial or ethnic membership. It is the belief that one race is inherently superior or inferior in dignity and value.

This is a sin. There is nothing controversial about that.

It denies the **_imago Dei_** — the image of God — in every human being. Genesis 1:27 is crystal clear: _“So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them.”_ Every human being, regardless of colour, carries the image of the Creator. To demean someone because of their race is to demean God Himself.

God shows no partiality. Peter declares this in Acts 10:34–35: _“Then Peter opened his mouth and said, ‘In truth I know that God is no respecter of persons; but in every nation He fears Me and does what is right, He is accepted by Him.’_” The apostle Paul echoes it in Romans 2:11: _“For there is no partiality with God.”_ And James 2:1 is merciless: _“My brethren, do not hold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons.”_

1980s racism — the belief that some humans are inherently less worthy because of their race — is a direct violation of Scripture. It is a sin against the image of God. It deserves to be called out, confronted, and repented of.

No one on this blog, no follower of Christ, should defend it.

## 2026 Racism

But the word has been hijacked. What people are calling “racism” in 2026 bears almost no resemblance to what it once meant. Let me call it “2026 racism” — a new, inflated, weaponized definition. It includes (not exhaustively):

- Any recognition of average group differences — cultural, behavioural, statistical.
- A preference for one’s own national or ethnic continuity.
- Critique of cultural practices that do not align with one’s values.
- Opposition to policies that seek to erase distinctions between peoples.
- Any resistance to the narrative that all groups are functionally identical in every measurable way.

This latter definition of racism is **not** a sin. In fact, it is not even wrong. It is, I would argue, thoroughly biblical.

Consider what Scripture actually says about nations and peoples.

**Acts 17:26** — _“And He has made from one blood all nations of men to dwell on all the face of the earth, having determined _beforehand_ the appointed seasons and _the_ bounds of their dwelling.”_ God did not make one amorphous global mush. He made nations — distinct, bounded, separate — and He determined their boundaries. He designed [human diversity](/diversity) into the fabric of creation.

**Deuteronomy 32:8** — _“According to the number of the sons of God, / So the assembly of Israel is a people alone.”_’ The full text speaks of God dividing the nations at Babel and assigning them. And Genesis 10 — the Table of Nations — confirms it. God scattered the peoples and gave them distinct inheritances.

**Titus 1:12–13** — _“One of themselves, a prophet of their own, said, ‘Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons.’ This testimony is true. Therefore rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith.”_’ Notice what Paul does. He endorses a cultural generalization about Cretans and uses it pastorally. He doesn’t backpedal. He says, “This testimony is true.” If observing cultural patterns makes you a racist, then Paul is a racist. But Paul is not a racist — he is an apostle.

To recognise differences between peoples is not to hate them. To prefer your own culture is not to despise another. To critique a policy or a practice is not to malign a race.

## The Church Is Not a Social Club

Here is the line we must draw and hold. The Church is not a committee. The Church is not a social club. The Church is not a mirror of current cultural consensus. The Church submits to God’s Word — even, and especially, when it puts us at odds with the culture around us.

And right now, the culture is telling us that noticing anything about race, nation, or culture is an unforgivable crime. The culture demands we swallow a narrative of functional human sameness and pretend that every group is interchangeable in every way. When we refuse — not out of hatred, but out of honesty — the culture calls us racists.

We must not accept that framing. We must not apologise for Scripture.

We must admonish and reprimand those who throw the term “racist” around in an accusatory manner to silence honest discussion. The apostle Paul rebukes, corrects, and confronts false teaching without hesitation. So should we.

**The Church does not set her agenda by what is popular. She sets her agenda by what is true.**

## The Real Racism

And here is the final irony — the one that the culture will not let you speak.

Actually, 1980s racism is still very much alive today. And it is explicitly directed against the white race.

White people are told they are the problem. White history is presented as a catalogue of crimes. White culture is systematically deconstructed and despised. White people are blamed for problems they did not create and are denied the right to preserve their heritage, their culture, their nation. A white person who speaks of their own racial identity is labelled a racist — while every other race celebrates theirs.

And if you question this? If you dare to say out loud what everyone can see? You are branded a racist. The word is turned upside down. The victim is the oppressor. The accuser is the accused. It is a complete inversion of reality, and it is being done with deliberate malice.

This madness — this poison of BLM, [leftism](/leftism), and critical race theory — must not be allowed to seep into the Church. It has no business in the house of God. It divides. It distracts. It discourages the Church from its mission. It is a weapon designed to destroy Western civilisation from within, and the Church must not become its unwitting accomplice.

We are called to preach the gospel, not to perform penance for a race we did not choose. We are called to unity in Christ, not to ideological conformity. We are called to truth — and the truth is that the word _racist_ has been hijacked, the narrative has been inverted, and the Church must not be silenced.

Come, Lord Jesus.
