A boat, eight, and a person. That is **船**, the character for ship: 舟 for the boat, 八 for eight, 口 for the mouth that makes a man. **Eight souls went into the ark, and the character still says so** (1 Peter 3:20).

**I did not find that.** Pastor **C. H. Kang** did, in a footnote to a missionary’s Mandarin textbook. He set 造 beside Genesis 2:7—**dust, a mouth, and a man on his feet**—and then spent forty years asking what the other characters had kept. *Genesis and the Chinese* came out in Hong Kong in 1950. *The Discovery of Genesis*, with Ethel R. Nelson, in 1979.

Three new books walk on from that work. **They are out now.**

## Two Chinese volumes

The first is the door: **155 characters, five a day, thirty-one days**, ten minutes each, from the first morning of Genesis to the gospel. Righteousness is **a lamb, 羊, standing over me, 我**. Rest is a person beside a tree, 休.

![Biblical Etymologies — Chinese Edition, Volume 1: a rose-red cover, the title in white capitals over a faint pattern of words](/assets/etymologies-2-vol1.jpg)

*Volume 1. Five easy characters a day for a child who does not yet know any Chinese, and the last days of it are the gospel.*

The second is **one character a day and one four-character saying**, told as the story it is. 塞翁之馬: the old man at the border, the horse that ran away, the horse that came home, the broken leg, the war. **The character is learned inside the tale.**

![Biblical Etymologies — Chinese Edition, Volume 2: a gold-to-olive cover with the title in white](/assets/etymologies-2-vol2.jpg)

*Volume 2. Thirty-one characters, thirty-one sayings, and the story of Genesis 1–11 walked one character at a time.*

Then it does the same work in Korean. 천지 is 天地. 천사 is 天使. 천재 is 天才. **One writing system, three languages**, and a child who already says the word.

## The one I wanted for myself

The third book gathers **all four languages** into one volume. **Five courses of thirty-one days**—Hebrew, Greek, Latin, and the two Chinese parts—412 pages, a verse in front of every lesson. It is the **student edition**: no questions, no answer keys, only the lessons and the appendices. Hebrew comes first, because the Bible opens in Hebrew.

![Etymologies — Hebrew, Greek, Latin, and Chinese: a deep purple cover with a dark title panel](/assets/etymologies-2-cover.jpg)

*One volume, four languages, five courses of thirty-one days. Published by Maranatha Harpazo.*

## The rule I would not bend

Every day opens on a verse, chosen the way it was in [the first three volumes](/etymologies): **Psalm 119** first wherever a real match existed; another psalm or a proverb if not; the rest of the Bible only when the match was exact.

> “The law of Your mouth is better to me than thousands of coins of gold and silver.”
>
> Psalm 119:72 (NKJV)

> “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge, but fools despise wisdom and instruction.”
>
> Proverbs 1:7 (NKJV)

**A book about words must not teach a child that words come first.**

## For whom

My own children are in secondary school here, and their Korean slips a little further every year. My nieces and nephews in California already knew a good deal of hanja. **This is where those two facts meet**—a character on one page, a word the child already speaks on the other.

I believe we are living at the end of the last days. I want the next generation of the Body of Christ found faithful and watchful, like the five wise virgins, with oil in their lamps—and the oil real. The press that publishes these books is named for that hope: **Maranatha Harpazo**. Our Lord, come.

The Korean edition of *Etymologies* is typeset, and it takes [the road Bible Promises took](/promises) rather than Amazon’s. When that road opens, it will be announced here.

Maranatha.
